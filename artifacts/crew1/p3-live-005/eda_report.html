Exploratory data analysis: eligible synthetic snapshots

ALL DATA IS SYNTHETIC. Descriptive analysis of the Policy C eligible snapshots in the Crew 1 handoff. Tables and charts are computed by deterministic tools from stats.json; the interpretation paragraphs are written by the Exploratory Data Analyst agent and are checked against the computed statistics. Nothing here is a model result or evidence of real-world accuracy.

Overview

The exploratory data analysis examines 1,538 eligible snapshots from 397 productions across four event stages (S0, S1, S2, S3) and three frozen split groups (train, test, demo). The data includes four event archetypes: bar/bat mitzvah, conference, corporate, and wedding. The signed gap is the verified final cost plus the contractual fee when included in the budget, minus the approved budget in effect at the snapshot (ILS, pre-VAT); a positive gap indicates over budget and a negative gap indicates under budget. The analysis measures overrun prevalence (the signed gap exceeds 0.5 ILS) and material overrun prevalence (the signed gap exceeds 2% of the budget).

Data quality and Policy C exclusions

Of 1,600 scheduled snapshots, 1,538 remained eligible after Policy C applied deterministically. Sixty-two snapshots became ineligible: 50 due to commitment_category_missing and 12 due to manual_cost_undated. Ineligibility was distributed across stages as S0: 3 snapshots, S1: 14 snapshots, S2: 20 snapshots, and S3: 25 snapshots. Among 400 productions, 375 were fully eligible, 3 were fully ineligible, and 22 had at least one ineligible snapshot. No productions were flagged as target_unreliable. The cleaning process identified 42 records with problems and dropped exact duplicates across three tables; normalisations were applied deterministically without imputation.

Budget revisions

Overrun prevalence is associated with the number of budget revisions to date. Snapshots with zero revisions show an overrun prevalence of 51.7% (95% interval: 49.0% to 54.4%), with a mean gap of 1,965.3 ILS. Snapshots with one revision show a prevalence of 25.0% (95% interval: 18.9% to 32.3%), with a mean gap of -19,638.0 ILS. Snapshots with two or more revisions show a prevalence of 9.8% (95% interval: 4.6% to 19.8%), with a mean gap of -39,325.5 ILS. The association suggests that snapshots with more revisions tend to have lower overrun prevalence and more negative gaps.

Fee mode and fee inclusion

Overrun prevalence by fee mode shows that snapshots with an amount-based fee have a prevalence of 52.6% (95% interval: 48.4% to 56.8%), while those with a percent-based fee have a prevalence of 44.5% (95% interval: 41.4% to 47.6%). By fee inclusion, snapshots where the fee is included in the budget show an overrun prevalence of 48.7% (95% interval: 45.3% to 52.1%), while those where the fee is not included show a prevalence of 45.8% (95% interval: 42.2% to 49.4%). The mean gap for snapshots with the fee included is -466.4 ILS, compared to -3,430.3 ILS when the fee is not included.

The test group has 306 snapshots from 80 productions with an overrun prevalence of 59.2% (95% bootstrap interval: 48.4% to 69.5%), the train group has 1,084 snapshots from 278 productions with a prevalence of 44.5% (95% interval: 38.9% to 50.2%), and the demo group has 148 snapshots from 39 productions with a prevalence of 43.9% (95% interval: 29.7% to 59.2%). The split is frozen, not tuned; this difference is a sampling outcome documented when the split was frozen.

Limitations

The analysis is descriptive and does not establish causation or predict outcomes. Correlations are computed on the train group only and are weak across all features, limiting their explanatory power. The frozen split difference, while statistically distinct from zero, reflects the sampling outcome at the time the split was frozen and should not be interpreted as evidence of systematic differences between groups. The signed gap and overrun definitions are based on the P2 contract and do not include payments or commitment totals. The analysis does not assess the real-world accuracy of the synthetic data or recommend re-splitting or rebalancing the frozen split.
