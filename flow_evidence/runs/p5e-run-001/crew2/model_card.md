# Model card: Crew 2 budget-overrun model (Flow, TRAIN-only)

> **ALL DATA IS SYNTHETIC.** Every output is an experimental / demo prediction, produced by the Flow's TRAIN-only Crew 2 step. It is not evidence about real productions.

## Purpose and intended use

The model estimates the probability that a synthetic event production ends above the budget in effect at a budget snapshot (`is_overrun`). It supports an academic demonstration of a production budget card. A secondary model estimates a material overrun (`is_material_overrun`, above 2%); it is reported only.

Out of scope: decisions about real productions, people, suppliers or clients; any use on real data. The model is not validated on real data.

## Model

- Candidate selected by the pre-registered rule: **LR** (lowest mean grouped-CV Brier), parameters {'C': 10.0}.
- Probabilities are the mean of 3 members, each a pipeline (standardised numeric features, one-hot categories) with a Platt calibrator fit on a group-disjoint held-out side.
- Features: the 27 approved snapshot features (F27), listed in `features.csv` and `features.schema.json`. Three are only approximations in the product (`lead_time_days`, `days_since_contract`, `n_substitutions_to_date`).

## Training data

- Source: Crew 1's validated file handoff (Policy C), 1,538 eligible snapshots.
- Fitting uses the train split only: 1,084 rows from 278 productions; `is_overrun` prevalence 44.5%.
- Test (306 rows) and demo (148 rows) labels were redacted before Crew 2 saw the data; they were never used.

## Evaluation

- Current run, TRAIN-only grouped CV (5 production-grouped folds): mean Brier 0.1884 ± 0.0147, PR-AUC 0.7570 ± 0.0779, ROC-AUC 0.7891 ± 0.0414.
- Reproducibility: exact reproduction of the approved P4 CV (see `evaluation_report.md`, A.6).
- Historical P4 TEST (cited, not recomputed; TEST was not reopened by this run): Brier 0.1751 [0.1471, 0.2045], PR-AUC 0.8880, expected calibration error 0.111.

## Limitations

- All data are synthetic; nothing here is evidence of real-world accuracy.
- The historical test group has an overrun prevalence of 59.2% against 44.5% on train, and the probabilities were not well calibrated there.
- The selected C = 10.0 is the upper boundary of the pre-registered grid; a larger value was never evaluated.
- `is_overrun` is a rounding-tolerance label, not a commercially material overrun.
- Outside the pinned reference environment, results are checked against tolerances, not exactly.

## Synthetic-data disclosure

Every production, budget, supplier figure and outcome is generated by the project's synthetic data generator. No real client, person or event is represented.

## Ethical considerations

- **Decision support only.** A probability is not a verdict about a producer's competence. Any real use would need human review of every flagged production and a documented appeal path.
- **Automation bias.** A confident score can crowd out the producer's knowledge of the event; the card should always show the underlying budget position next to the score.
- **Fairness across segments.** Error rates differ by stage and could differ by event archetype. A real deployment would need per-segment evaluation on real data before use, and should not be used to price or rank clients or suppliers.
- **Calibration and drift.** The historical test showed miscalibration under a prevalence shift; scores must not be read as exact probabilities, and a real system would need monitoring and recalibration.
- **Privacy.** The data are synthetic and hold no personal data. A real system would hold commercially sensitive budgets and would need access control and data minimisation.
- **Transparency.** The selection rule was pre-registered and applied by code; TEST was evaluated once in P4 and never reopened; the protocol and its limits are disclosed here.
