Exploratory data analysis: eligible synthetic snapshots

ALL DATA IS SYNTHETIC. Descriptive analysis of the Policy C eligible snapshots in the Crew 1 handoff. Tables and charts are computed by deterministic tools from stats.json; the interpretation paragraphs are written by the Exploratory Data Analyst agent and are checked against the computed statistics. Nothing here is a model result or evidence of real-world accuracy.

Overview

This exploratory data analysis examines 1,538 eligible snapshots from 397 productions across four event stages (S0–S3) and three frozen split groups (train, test, demo). The analysis applies the P2 contract definitions of the signed gap (verified final cost plus contractual fee when included, minus approved budget) and overrun status (gap exceeding 0.5 ILS). The dataset includes 375 fully eligible productions, 22 partially eligible productions, and 3 fully ineligible productions from the 400 scheduled. Eight approved analyses were run to characterize overrun prevalence, budget gap distribution, budget revision effects, feature correlations, fee terms, and frozen split differences.

Data quality and Policy C exclusions

Policy C eligibility screening excluded 62 snapshots from the 1,600 scheduled: 50 due to missing commitment categories and 12 due to undated manual costs. Ineligibility increased progressively by stage, from 3 ineligible snapshots in S0 to 25 in S3. The cleaning process identified 42 records with problems across seven tables and removed exact duplicates and invalid payments. All normalisable data were preserved; only records that could not be reliably reconstructed under Policy C's deterministic rules were excluded. No target_unreliable_productions were flagged.

Overrun prevalence

Overall overrun prevalence (is_overrun, gap exceeding 0.5 ILS) was 47.3% (95% Wilson interval: 44.8%–49.8%) across the 1,538 eligible snapshots. Overrun prevalence declined across stages: S0 at 51.6% (95% interval: 46.7%–56.5%), S1 at 49.7% (95% interval: 44.8%–54.7%), S2 at 44.7% (95% interval: 39.8%–49.8%), and S3 at 42.9% (95% interval: 38.0%–48.0%). Material overrun prevalence (gap exceeding 2% of budget) was 35.7% (95% interval: 33.3%–38.1%) overall. By event type, conference snapshots showed the highest overrun prevalence at 55.1% (95% interval: 50.1%–60.0%), while wedding snapshots showed the lowest at 39.3% (95% interval: 34.5%–44.2%).

Signed budget gap

The signed gap distribution was right-skewed with a median of −988.9 ILS (under budget) and a mean of −1,863.6 ILS. The 5th to 95th percentile range was −46,958.0 ILS to 41,310.1 ILS. By stage, median gaps shifted from positive in S0 (282.4 ILS) to increasingly negative in S1 (−255.7 ILS), S2 (−2,535.0 ILS), and S3 (−3,441.9 ILS). The gap as a share of the as-of budget had a median of −0.5% and a 5th to 95th percentile range of −16.0% to 16.3%. The histogram shows concentration near zero with a long right tail, indicating most snapshots cluster near budget but some have substantial positive gaps.

Budget revisions

Overrun prevalence was strongly associated with the number of budget revisions to date. Snapshots with zero revisions had an overrun prevalence of 51.7% (95% interval: 49.0%–54.4%) and a mean gap of 1,965.3 ILS. Snapshots with one revision had a prevalence of 25.0% (95% interval: 18.9%–32.3%) and a mean gap of −19,638.0 ILS. Snapshots with two or more revisions had a prevalence of 9.8% (95% interval: 4.6%–19.8%) and a mean gap of −39,325.5 ILS. This association suggests that revisions are associated with larger negative gaps and lower overrun rates.

Fee mode and fee inclusion

Overrun prevalence differed by fee inclusion status. Snapshots with fees included in the budget had an overrun prevalence of 48.7% (95% interval: 45.3%–52.1%), while those without fees included had a prevalence of 45.8% (95% interval: 42.2%–49.4%). By fee mode, snapshots with amount-based fees had an overrun prevalence of 52.6% (95% interval: 48.4%–56.8%), while those with percent-based fees had a prevalence of 44.5% (95% interval: 41.4%–47.6%). Mean gaps were −466.4 ILS for fees included and −3,430.3 ILS for fees not included, and −1,585.5 ILS for amount-based fees versus −2,013.2 ILS for percent-based fees.

Feature correlations (train group)

On the train group (1,084 snapshots), Spearman correlation with the signed gap showed the strongest associations with over_plan_pre_vat (0.266), budget_revisions_to_date (−0.246), and current_position_ils (0.124). Point-biserial correlation with is_overrun showed the strongest associations with over_plan_pre_vat (0.275), budget_revisions_to_date (−0.183), and manual_costs_pre_vat (0.176). All correlations were weak to moderate. Committed_ratio, guest_count, and lead_time_days showed weak correlations with both targets. No feature showed a strong linear or monotonic relationship with the gap or overrun status.

Frozen split prevalence difference

The frozen split showed a significant difference in overrun prevalence between test and train groups. Train group overrun prevalence was 44.5% (95% production-level bootstrap interval: 38.9%–50.2%) across 1,084 snapshots from 278 productions. Test group prevalence was 59.2% (95% interval: 48.4%–69.5%) across 306 snapshots from 80 productions. Demo group prevalence was 43.9% (95% interval: 29.7%–59.2%) across 148 snapshots from 39 productions. The test minus train difference was 14.7 percentage points (95% bootstrap interval: 3.3–26.2 pp), indicating the test group had higher overrun prevalence than the train group.

Limitations

This analysis describes associations in the eligible snapshots only; ineligible snapshots (62 total) are not represented. The frozen split is a documented sampling outcome, not a result of analysis, and the test group's higher overrun prevalence is an observed difference, not a tuning outcome. Correlations are computed on the train group only and are weak to moderate; no feature shows a strong predictive relationship with the gap or overrun status. The analysis does not assess causation, real-world accuracy, or the reliability of the underlying cost and budget data. Budget revisions, fee terms, and event type show associations with overrun prevalence, but these are descriptive findings, not explanations of overrun drivers.
